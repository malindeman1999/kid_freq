TLS Saturation Analysis of Al KID Power Sweeps

This page describes an analysis procedure for Al KID measurements where the internal quality factor \(Q_i\) and resonance frequency \(f_r\) are measured as functions of readout drive power and bath temperature. The goal is to test whether moderate-power increases in \(Q_i\) and upward shifts in \(f_r\) are consistent with saturation of two-level systems (TLS) in dielectric/interface regions of the resonator.

The motivating observation is:

  • As readout drive power increases, \(Q_i\) increases at moderate powers.
  • The fitted resonance frequency \(f_r\) also shifts upward at moderate powers.
  • The effect is strong at low temperature, including data near 50 mK.
  • This sign is opposite ordinary quasiparticle heating, which usually gives \(L_k \uparrow\), \(f_r \downarrow\), and \(Q_i \downarrow\).

This pattern is naturally consistent with TLS saturation:

\[ P_{\rm drive}\uparrow \quad \Rightarrow \quad \text{TLS saturation} \quad \Rightarrow \quad \tan\delta_{\rm TLS}\downarrow \quad \Rightarrow \quad Q_i\uparrow . \]

The same TLS population also produces dielectric dispersion. If TLS saturation reduces the effective dielectric constant, then the capacitance decreases and the resonance frequency increases:

\[ \epsilon_{\rm eff}\downarrow \quad \Rightarrow \quad C_{\rm eff}\downarrow \quad \Rightarrow \quad f_r\uparrow . \]

1. Data Available

For each resonator \(i\), temperature \(T_j\), and drive power \(P_k\), the analysis assumes the following fitted quantities are available:

  • \(Q_i(P_k,T_j)\): internal quality factor.
  • \(f_r(P_k,T_j)\): resonance frequency.
  • \(Q_r(P_k,T_j)\): loaded quality factor, if available.
  • \(Q_c(P_k,T_j)\): coupling quality factor, if available.
  • \(a(P_k,T_j)\): nonlinear resonator parameter from the Swenson-style nonlinear fit, if available.
  • Resonator design metadata: design group, frequency, geometry, wafer position, etc.

The primary TLS analysis should be done using loss:

\[ y(P,T) = Q_i^{-1}(P,T), \]

not directly using \(Q_i\), because independent loss mechanisms add approximately linearly in \(Q^{-1}\).

2. TLS Loss Saturation Model

2.1 Fixed-temperature model

At a single temperature and for one resonator, fit the power dependence of the internal loss to:

\[ Q_i^{-1}(P_{\rm drive}) = Q_{\rm other}^{-1} + \frac{A_{\rm TLS}(T,f)} {\left[1+\left(P_{\rm drive}/P_c\right)^\beta\right]^{1/2}}. \]

Here:

Parameter Meaning
\(Q_{\rm other}\) Residual non-saturating internal quality factor. It is the high-power asymptote if TLS saturates before heating or other nonlinear effects dominate.
\(A_{\rm TLS}(T,f)\) Numerator of the TLS loss term at a given temperature and resonator frequency. This is the apparent unsaturated TLS loss amplitude at that \(T\) and \(f\).
\(P_c\) Critical saturation power expressed at the chosen drive-power reference plane. This equals the true internal TLS critical power divided by the power gain/buildup factor.
\(\beta\) Empirical saturation exponent. Ideal uniform-field TLS saturation suggests \(\beta \approx 1\), but real devices often give smaller effective values because of nonuniform fields and TLS distributions.
Important: \(P_c\) is not the microscopic TLS critical power unless the attenuation, coupling, and internal resonator buildup are known. From a fit versus raw drive power, the result is \(P_c/G\), where \(G\) is the effective conversion from drive power to internal resonator power.

2.2 Temperature and frequency dependence of the numerator

In the standard TLS model, the numerator should be:

\[ A_{\rm TLS}(T,f) = F\tan\delta_0 \, \tanh\left(\frac{hf}{2k_B T}\right). \]

Here:

Parameter Meaning
\(F\) Dielectric participation factor of the TLS-hosting region.
\(\tan\delta_0\) Intrinsic low-power, low-temperature TLS loss tangent.
\(F\tan\delta_0\) Effective TLS loss amplitude. This is usually what the resonator data determine, not \(F\) and \(\tan\delta_0\) separately.
\(\tanh(hf/2k_BT)\) Thermal population factor for resonant TLS absorption at resonator frequency \(f\).

Therefore the measured numerator \(A_{\rm TLS}(T,f)\) should:

  • increase as temperature decreases,
  • increase as resonator frequency increases,
  • approximately collapse to a constant after dividing by \(\tanh(hf/2k_BT)\), assuming similar TLS participation.

Define the corrected TLS amplitude:

\[ A_{\rm corr}(T,f) = \frac{A_{\rm TLS}(T,f)} {\tanh\left(\frac{hf}{2k_BT}\right)}. \]

The TLS model predicts:

\[ A_{\rm corr}(T,f) \approx F\tan\delta_0. \]

Thus, after correction, \(A_{\rm corr}\) should be approximately independent of temperature and frequency, apart from resonator-to-resonator participation differences, fit errors, or additional loss mechanisms.

3. Useful Temperature and Frequency Scale

A useful conversion is:

\[ \frac{hf}{k_B} \approx 48\,{\rm mK}\times f_{\rm GHz}. \]

Therefore:

\[ \frac{hf}{2k_BT} \approx 0.48 \frac{f_{\rm GHz}}{T/50\,{\rm mK}}. \]

Example values:

Temperature \(\tanh(hf/2k_BT)\), 1 GHz \(\tanh(hf/2k_BT)\), 2 GHz
50 mK 0.45 0.74
100 mK 0.24 0.45
200 mK 0.12 0.24
300 mK 0.080 0.16

In the high-temperature limit \(k_BT \gg hf\),

\[ \tanh\left(\frac{hf}{2k_BT}\right) \approx \frac{hf}{2k_BT}. \]

So in that limit:

\[ A_{\rm TLS}(T,f) \propto \frac{f}{T}. \]

4. Global Fit Model

For one resonator over all powers and temperatures, a useful model is:

\[ Q_i^{-1}(P,T) = Q_{\rm other}^{-1}(T) + \frac{ F\tan\delta_0 \, \tanh\left(\frac{hf}{2k_BT}\right) } { \left[1+\left(P_{\rm drive}/P_c\right)^\beta\right]^{1/2} }. \]

In this model:

  • \(F\tan\delta_0\), \(P_c\), and \(\beta\) may be treated as resonator-level parameters.
  • \(Q_{\rm other}^{-1}(T)\) may be treated as a nuisance background that varies with temperature.
  • If the fit is unstable, \(\beta\) can be shared globally across resonators or across a design group.

For resonator \(i\), temperature \(T_j\), and power \(P_k\), write:

\[ y_{ijk} = B_{ij} + \frac{ A_i \, \tanh\left(\frac{hf_i}{2k_BT_j}\right) } { \left[1+\left(P_k/P_{c,i}\right)^\beta\right]^{1/2} }, \]

where:

\[ y_{ijk}=Q_{i}^{-1}(P_k,T_j), \] \[ B_{ij}=Q_{{\rm res},i}^{-1}(T_j), \] \[ A_i=F_i\tan\delta_0. \]

5. Recommended Fitting Strategy

5.1 First pass: fit each temperature independently

For each resonator and each temperature, fit:

\[ Q_i^{-1}(P) = B + \frac{A_T} {\left[1+\left(P/P_c\right)^\beta\right]^{1/2}}. \]

Save the fitted parameters:

  • \(B = Q_{\rm other}^{-1}\)
  • \(A_T = A_{\rm TLS}(T,f)\)
  • \(P_c\)
  • \(\beta\)
  • fit uncertainty and fit quality metrics

Then test:

\[ A_T \stackrel{?}{\propto} \tanh\left(\frac{hf}{2k_BT}\right). \]

5.2 Second pass: fit all temperatures for one resonator

For each resonator, fit all powers and temperatures simultaneously:

\[ Q_i^{-1}(P,T) = B(T) + \frac{ A_0 \, \tanh\left(\frac{hf}{2k_BT}\right) } { \left[1+\left(P/P_c\right)^\beta\right]^{1/2} }. \]

Here \(A_0=F\tan\delta_0\). \(B(T)\) can initially be one free value per temperature, although this is a nuisance-rich fit. Later, \(B(T)\) can be replaced by a smoother model if justified.

5.3 Third pass: array-level fit

For many resonators, use shared parameters where physically reasonable:

  • Use one global \(\beta\), or one \(\beta\) per design group.
  • Allow \(F_i\tan\delta_0\) to vary per resonator.
  • Allow \(P_{c,i}\) to vary per resonator unless internal photon number normalization is used.
  • Allow \(Q_{{\rm res},i}^{-1}(T)\) to vary with resonator and temperature.
Best robust output: report both the fitted \(F\tan\delta_0\) and the observed saturable loss over the measured power range: \[ \Delta Q_{\rm sat}^{-1} = Q_i^{-1}(P_{\rm low})-Q_i^{-1}(P_{\rm high}). \] The second quantity is less model-dependent.

6. Internal Power and Photon Number

Fitting versus raw drive power gives \(P_c\), not the true internal TLS saturation power. The conversion from feedline power to resonator internal energy depends on attenuation, coupling, loaded quality factor, and resonance frequency.

A useful approximate internal photon number scaling for a notch resonator is:

\[ \bar n \propto \frac{P_{\rm feed} Q_r^2}{\hbar \omega_r^2 Q_c}. \]

The exact prefactor depends on convention and resonator model. However, the important point is that the internal photon number is not simply proportional to raw source power. It varies with:

  • \(Q_r\)
  • \(Q_c\)
  • \(\omega_r = 2\pi f_r\)
  • line attenuation and gain calibration

If possible, refit the TLS saturation model using \(\bar n\) instead of \(P_{\rm drive}\):

\[ Q_i^{-1}(\bar n,T) = Q_{\rm other}^{-1}(T) + \frac{ F\tan\delta_0 \, \tanh\left(\frac{hf}{2k_BT}\right) } { \left[1+\left(\bar n/n_c\right)^\beta\right]^{1/2} }. \]

Then \(n_c\) is the effective critical photon number for TLS saturation.

7. Frequency Shift Analysis

TLS affects not only loss but also the real dielectric response. The resonant frequency is:

\[ f_r \approx \frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{LC}}. \]

For small shifts:

\[ \frac{\Delta f_r}{f_r} \approx -\frac{1}{2}\frac{\Delta L}{L} -\frac{1}{2}\frac{\Delta C}{C}. \]

TLS saturation is expected to reduce the TLS contribution to the effective dielectric constant:

\[ P_{\rm drive}\uparrow \quad \Rightarrow \quad \epsilon_{\rm TLS}\downarrow \quad \Rightarrow \quad C_{\rm eff}\downarrow \quad \Rightarrow \quad f_r\uparrow. \]

Quasiparticle heating or nonlinear kinetic inductance usually gives the opposite frequency sign:

\[ P_{\rm drive}\uparrow \quad \Rightarrow \quad L_k\uparrow \quad \Rightarrow \quad f_r\downarrow. \]

A phenomenological power-dependent frequency model is:

\[ \frac{\Delta f_r(P,T)}{f_r} = x_0(T) + A_f(T) \left[ 1- \frac{1} {\left[1+\left(P/P_{c,f}\right)^\gamma\right]^{1/2}} \right] + A_a a(P,T) + x_{\rm other}(P,T). \]

In this expression:

  • The \(A_f\) term represents upward TLS capacitance saturation.
  • The \(A_a a(P,T)\) term represents the contribution correlated with the Swenson nonlinear parameter.
  • \(x_{\rm other}\) represents heating, quasiparticles, fit artifacts, or other effects.

A strong TLS signature is:

\[ P_{c,f} \approx P_{c,Q}, \]

meaning that the power scale for frequency saturation matches the power scale for \(Q_i\) improvement.

8. Role of the Swenson Nonlinear Parameter \(a\)

The nonlinear parameter \(a\) from the Swenson-style resonator fit is a useful diagnostic for inductive nonlinearity and line-shape distortion. It should be compared against the measured frequency shift and loss improvement.

Define:

\[ \Delta x(P,T) = \frac{f_r(P,T)-f_r(P_{\rm low},T)}{f_r(P_{\rm low},T)}. \]

Then make scatter plots:

  • \(\Delta x\) versus \(a\)
  • \(\Delta Q_i^{-1}\) versus \(a\)
  • \(\Delta x\) versus \(\Delta Q_i^{-1}\)

where:

\[ \Delta Q_i^{-1} = Q_i^{-1}(P_{\rm low},T) - Q_i^{-1}(P,T). \]

Expected signatures:

Observation Interpretation
\(\Delta x>0\), \(Q_i\uparrow\), weak correlation with \(a\) Likely TLS capacitance/loss saturation.
\(\Delta x<0\), strong correlation with \(a\) Likely kinetic-inductance nonlinearity or heating-dominated behavior.
\(\Delta x>0\), strong correlation with \(\Delta Q_i^{-1}\) Strong TLS evidence: same saturating mechanism affects loss and dispersion.
Frequency shifts upward at moderate power but turns downward at high power Possible crossover from TLS saturation to heating or kinetic-inductance nonlinearity.

9. Required Diagnostic Plots

9.1 Per-resonator power sweeps

  • Plot \(Q_i^{-1}\) versus \(P_{\rm drive}\) for each temperature.
  • Plot fitted TLS saturation curves over the data.
  • Plot residuals versus power.
  • Mark any powers excluded because of bifurcation, distorted fits, or heating.

9.2 TLS numerator versus model factor

For each fitted temperature slice, plot:

\[ A_{\rm TLS}(T,f) \quad \text{versus} \quad u(T,f) = \tanh\left(\frac{hf}{2k_BT}\right). \]

TLS prediction:

\[ A_{\rm TLS}=F\tan\delta_0 \, u. \]

So the plot should be approximately linear through the origin for each resonator or design group.

9.3 Corrected numerator

Plot:

\[ A_{\rm corr} = \frac{A_{\rm TLS}} {\tanh\left(\frac{hf}{2k_BT}\right)} \]

versus:

  • temperature,
  • resonator frequency,
  • resonator index,
  • design group,
  • wafer position.

TLS prediction: \(A_{\rm corr}\) should be much flatter versus temperature and frequency than the raw numerator.

9.4 Saturation power

Plot:

  • \(P_c\) versus resonator frequency,
  • \(P_c\) versus \(Q_r\),
  • \(P_c\) versus \(Q_c\),
  • critical photon number \(n_c\), if available, versus frequency.

If fitting versus raw drive power, apparent frequency trends in \(P_c\) may be caused by coupling and resonator energy buildup rather than intrinsic TLS physics.

9.5 Frequency-shift checks

  • Plot \(\Delta f_r/f_r\) versus \(P_{\rm drive}\) for each temperature.
  • Compare the frequency saturation power to the \(Q_i\) saturation power.
  • Plot \(\Delta f_r/f_r\) versus \(\Delta Q_i^{-1}\).
  • Plot \(\Delta f_r/f_r\) versus nonlinear parameter \(a\).

10. Fit Quality and Failure Modes

10.1 Good TLS-like behavior

A strong TLS interpretation is supported if:

  • \(Q_i^{-1}\) decreases with power at low/moderate powers.
  • The decrease has a saturating shape.
  • The fitted numerator \(A_{\rm TLS}\) follows \(\tanh(hf/2k_BT)\).
  • The corrected numerator \(A_{\rm corr}\) is flatter versus \(T\) and \(f\).
  • The resonance frequency shifts upward over the same power range.
  • The frequency and loss saturation powers are similar.
  • The upward frequency shift correlates better with \(\Delta Q_i^{-1}\) than with the nonlinear parameter \(a\).

10.2 Warning signs

Be cautious if:

  • The high-power data do not reach a plateau.
  • \(Q_i\) eventually decreases at high power, indicating heating or excess quasiparticles.
  • The resonance curves are strongly bifurcated or distorted.
  • The fitted \(Q_i\), \(f_r\), or \(a\) are strongly correlated in the nonlinear fit covariance.
  • The extracted \(\beta\) varies wildly with temperature.
  • The fitted \(A_{\rm TLS}\) does not decrease with temperature.
  • The corrected \(A_{\rm corr}\) becomes more temperature-dependent than the raw numerator.
Important caveat: If the power sweep only covers the rising part of \(Q_i(P)\) and does not bracket the saturation rollover or high-power asymptote, then \(Q_{\rm other}\), \(A_{\rm TLS}\), \(P_c\), and \(\beta\) can become highly correlated. In that case, report the observed saturable loss over the measured range as a robust lower-bound-like quantity.

11. Suggested Code Structure

11.1 Input table

Use a long-form table with one row per resonator, temperature, and power: 

resonator_id
design_group
temperature_K
drive_power_dBm
drive_power_linear_W
frequency_Hz
Qi
Qr
Qc
a_nonlinear
fit_quality_flags

11.2 Derived columns

loss = 1 / Qi
f_GHz = frequency_Hz / 1e9
tls_u = tanh(h * frequency_Hz / (2 * k_B * temperature_K))

11.3 Fixed-temperature fit function

def tls_loss_vs_power(P, B, A, Pc, beta):
    return B + A / sqrt(1 + (P / Pc)**beta)

where:

  • B is \(Q_{\rm other}^{-1}\),
  • A is \(A_{\rm TLS}(T,f)\),
  • Pc is \(P_c\),
  • beta is the saturation exponent.

11.4 Multi-temperature fit function

def tls_loss_vs_power_temp(P, T, f, B_of_T, A0, Pc, beta):
    u = tanh(h * f / (2 * k_B * T))
    return B_of_T[T] + A0 * u / sqrt(1 + (P / Pc)**beta)

In practical code, B_of_T can be represented as one fitted nuisance parameter per temperature, or as a smooth function of temperature.

11.5 Corrected numerator

A_corr = A_fit / tanh(h * frequency_Hz / (2 * k_B * temperature_K))

12. Recommended Outputs

For each resonator, save:

  • \(A_{\rm TLS}(T)\) from fixed-temperature fits.
  • \(A_{\rm corr}(T)\).
  • Best-fit \(F\tan\delta_0\) from multi-temperature fit.
  • \(P_c\) or \(n_c\), if photon number normalization is available.
  • \(\beta\).
  • \(Q_{\rm other}(T)\).
  • Fit residual RMS and reduced \(\chi^2\), if uncertainties are available.
  • Flags for bad fits, high-power heating, bifurcation, or missing plateau.

For the array, save:

  • Median \(F\tan\delta_0\) by design group.
  • Distribution of \(F\tan\delta_0\).
  • Distribution of \(\beta\).
  • Distribution of \(P_c\) or \(n_c\).
  • Correlation of \(\Delta f_r/f_r\) with \(\Delta Q_i^{-1}\).
  • Correlation of \(\Delta f_r/f_r\) with nonlinear parameter \(a\).

13. Interpretation Summary

If the TLS model is correct, then the power-dependent loss should be described by:

\[ Q_i^{-1}(P,T) = Q_{\rm other}^{-1}(T) + \frac{ F\tan\delta_0 \, \tanh\left(\frac{hf}{2k_BT}\right) } { \left[1+\left(P/P_c\right)^\beta\right]^{1/2} }. \]

The strongest evidence for TLS would be:

  • The numerator of the power-saturating term follows \(\tanh(hf/2k_BT)\).
  • After dividing by \(\tanh(hf/2k_BT)\), the inferred \(F\tan\delta_0\) is relatively flat versus temperature and frequency.
  • The resonance frequency shifts upward over the same power range where \(Q_i\) improves.
  • The frequency-shift saturation power agrees with the loss-saturation power.
  • The effect is strongest at low temperature.

This combination supports the interpretation that the moderate-power increase in \(Q_i\) and upward frequency shift are dominated by TLS saturation in the capacitive/dielectric part of the resonator, rather than quasiparticle heating or nonlinear kinetic inductance.
